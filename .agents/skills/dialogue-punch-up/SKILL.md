# dialogue-punch-up

Rewrite screenplay dialogue for sharper wit, stronger character voice, better subtext, and cleaner rhythm — without changing what the scene needs to accomplish.

## What this skill improves

- **On-the-nose dialogue** — characters saying exactly what they mean/feel
- **Flat exposition** — information delivered without conflict or personality
- **Rhythm and length** — lines that are too long, too similar in cadence, or too clean
- **Voice differentiation** — characters who all sound like the same writer
- **Wit and surprise** — replacing expected responses with the unexpected-but-inevitable

## Workflow

1. Ask the user to paste the scene or dialogue excerpt.
2. Ask: What is the dramatic purpose of this scene? What does each character want in this moment?
3. Ask: Any notes on character voice, dialect, education level, verbal tics?
4. Produce the punched-up version in proper screenplay format.
5. Annotate key changes with a brief note explaining the craft choice (optional, on request).

## Punch-up principles

**Cut to subtext.** Characters rarely say what they mean. Find the oblique version.

**Before:**
> MARCUS: I'm angry at you because you lied to me.

**After:**
> MARCUS: You want me to pass the salt?

---

**Find the button.** The last line of an exchange should land or cut — not trail off.

**Vary the rhythm.** Short lines hit hard. Mix them with longer ones to control pace.

**Earn the joke.** Wit should come from character, not from the writer being clever.

**Read aloud test.** If you can't say it fast, actors can't either. Cut it.

## Output format

Provide the rewritten scene in standard screenplay format:

```
INT. KITCHEN - NIGHT

MARCUS sets two plates on the table. Doesn't look at her.

SARA
I should have told you.

MARCUS
You want salt?

SARA
Marcus—

MARCUS
There's pepper too. Full range of condiments.
```

Offer to do multiple passes with different tonal registers (dryer, warmer, funnier, darker) on request.
