# pitch-treatment

Write compelling pitch treatments for TV series, limited series, and feature films.

## TV Series Treatment (standard sections)

1. **Title & Tagline**
2. **Logline** — one sentence: protagonist + world + central engine + stakes
3. **The Show** — 2–3 paragraphs: tone, world, why now, what makes it undeniable
4. **The Engine** — what drives episode-to-episode storytelling; the repeatable dramatic question
5. **Characters**
   - Series lead: want, need, wound, voice
   - 3–5 supporting characters: role and relationship to lead
6. **Tone & Comp Titles** — "This show lives at the intersection of X and Y" + 2–3 comps (recent, successful, not too on-the-nose)
7. **Season Arc** — beginning, midpoint break, season finale shape (no spoilers needed, just dramatic shape)
8. **Episode Breakdown** — pilot + 4–6 episode springboards (title + 2–3 sentences each)
9. **Writer's Vision** — personal connection, thematic intention, why this writer for this story

## Film Treatment (adapted sections)

1. **Title & Logline**
2. **The World & Tone**
3. **Characters** (protagonist, antagonist, key supporting)
4. **Act Breakdown** — act one setup, midpoint, act two turn, climax shape (no full spoilers)
5. **Themes**
6. **Comp Titles & Market Position**
7. **Writer's Vision**

## Workflow

1. Ask: Is this TV or film? What genre/tone? Who is the audience (network, streamer, indie)?
2. Ask for any existing materials (logline, outline, character notes).
3. Write the full treatment in polished prose — not bullet points in final form.
4. Keep TV treatments to 8–15 pages; film treatments to 5–10 pages.
5. Offer a one-page "leave-behind" version on request.

## Tone

- Write in present tense, active voice, with the energy of the show itself.
- Avoid passive constructions and hedging language.
- The treatment should feel like the show; match genre voice (e.g., a horror treatment should unsettle).
